autoscaler An 8× spike, scaling 2 → 16 pods

Meeting traffic jumps ~8× on the hour. The fleet scales on queue depth - requests waiting - not GPU utilisation, because utilisation pins at 100% whether 2 or 200 are queued. This replays the recorded KEDA reaction: scale-up begins one 2-second sample after the spike lands.

latency ⇄ throughput One dial, two goals that fight

Pack more requests per GPU and total throughput rises while each caller waits longer. No single point gives both - a fleet reconciles them by keeping each pod at low concurrency and adding pods.

predictive scaling A traffic forecaster, 10 minutes ahead

A LightGBM model trained on Microsoft's real Azure Functions 2019 trace (13.5 billion requests, ~47k functions, 14 days). Shown on a held-out day it never saw during training. Predicting ahead lets the fleet warm pods before load arrives, instead of reacting after.

3.3%forecast error (MAPE), 10 min ahead
33%lower error than the best naive baseline
23%fewer requests left short of capacity vs reactive, at equal cost

Honest note: on smooth aggregate traffic the gain is modest; predictive scaling pays off most on sharp, synchronised spikes. The reactive queue-depth autoscaler above still catches the surprises a forecast can't.
